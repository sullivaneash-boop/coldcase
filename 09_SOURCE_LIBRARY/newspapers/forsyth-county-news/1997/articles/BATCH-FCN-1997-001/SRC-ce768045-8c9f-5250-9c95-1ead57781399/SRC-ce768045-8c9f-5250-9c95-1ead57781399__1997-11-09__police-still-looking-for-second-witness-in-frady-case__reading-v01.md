## Full extant article text

<!-- P001 -->
After talking to one of the men depicted in two sketches released Wednesday, authorities investigating the murder of Levi Frady still don’t know the identity of the other man in that sketch and now have a third man they would like to locate.

<!-- P002 -->
Although they now have a sketch of yet another “potential witness,” it may be early next week before that artist’s rendering is released — if it’s released at all.

<!-- P003 -->
Georgia Bureau of Investigation special agent-in-charge Jim Hallman said it may not be necessary to seek the public’s help in identifying the man in the latest drawing. “We have an idea we may know who that man is,” he said.

<!-- P004 -->
The latest sketch was prepared Thursday, after a man police talked to during a roadblock came forward to say he had noticed someone who seemed suspicious near the wooded area where the 11-year-old’s body was found.

<!-- P005 -->
Hallman said none of the three men depicted in the sketches were seen together and are only considered to be potential witnesses. They are not being characterized as suspects, he said.

<!-- P006 -->
As for the man investigators identified and interviewed Wednesday night, Hallman won’t say whether agents learned anything significant and declined to release the man’s name.

<!-- P007 -->
He will only say that agents discovered his identity from the “development of a (vehicle) tag number” and went to his home in Forsyth County on the evening the sketch was released.

<!-- P008 -->
Hallman said the man had not seen the sketch of himself and was not aware he was being sought in the case.

<!-- P009 -->
The as-yet-unidentified white male in the sketch released Nov. 5 was also seen in the area where Levi’s body was found. He was reportedly driving a 1980-85 model medium blue Toyota pickup. Hallman wouldn’t say why the person who described the man considered him to be suspicious.

<!-- P010 -->
Hallman said “the case is solvable” and investigators are examining every lead “looking for a break,” saying authorities still want the public’s help. “We would be happy to pay the reward (now more than $15,000) to the person who know something about this killer,” he said.

<!-- P011 -->

<!-- P012 -->
Though television news reports have suggested investigators view revenge against one of Levi’s relatives as a likely motive, Hallman said that report did not come from official sources. He would not discuss possible motives.

<!-- P013 -->
Likewise, Hallman would not confirm reports that crime lab technicians found fingerprints at the crime scene, saying that information did not come from authorities. “I haven’t heard anything about prints from the crime lab this week,” he said.

<!-- P014 -->
Meanwhile, as of the Forsyth County News’ Friday night deadline, Levi’s father, Milton Frady, remained incarcerated in the county detention center on unrelated charges. Following a Wednesday bond hearing, Frady’s attorney, Michael Hauptman, had said he expected his client would be released by Friday.

<!-- P015 -->
At that hearing, Superior Court Judge Frank Mills indicated he would approve a bond — provided Frady could locate an electronic monitoring company willing to fit him with a personal surveillance device. Hauptman said the company which normally provides that service locally doesn’t want to get involved in “high-profile cases” but said he felt certain he could locate another company to take the case.

<!-- P016 -->
Mills directed District Attorney Garry Moss to work out the terms of the bond with Hauptman. A previous report erred in stating Judge Stan Gault presided at that hearing. Frady is being held on a 1995 bench warrant for failure to appear to stand trial for his alleged involvement in a 1994 burglary.
