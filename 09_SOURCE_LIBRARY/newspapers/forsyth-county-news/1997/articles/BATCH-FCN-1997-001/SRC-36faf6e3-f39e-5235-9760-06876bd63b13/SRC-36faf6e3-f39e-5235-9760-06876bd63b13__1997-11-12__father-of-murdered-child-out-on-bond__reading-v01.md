## Full extant article text

<!-- P001 -->
Following a week of negotiations, the father of 11-year-old murder victim Levi Frady was released from the Forsyth County Detention Center Monday.

<!-- P002 -->
Superior Court Judge Frank Mills ordered Milton Frady to wear an electronic monitoring system and remain at the Nichols Road home of his parents, Lamar and Sara Frady, unless appearing in court.

<!-- P003 -->
Frady turned himself in to the Sheriff’s Office Oct. 29. He was wanted for failing to appear in court in 1995 to answer to charges of burglary and contributing to the delinquency of a minor.

<!-- P004 -->
Investigators from the Forsyth and Dawson county sheriff’s departments and the Georgia Bureau Investigation wanted to question him about the Oct. 22 murder of his son, whose body was found Oct. 23 in a Dawson County forest by three hunters from Gilmer County. Levi disappeared Oct. 22 and family and neighbors searched north Forsyth County for the boy.

<!-- P005 -->
[Continued on page 2A under BOND from 1A]

<!-- P006 -->
Milton Frady did not attend the Nov. 2 funeral of his son. At the time, Sara Frady said her son was worried about being arrested for the outstanding warrants.

<!-- P007 -->
Milton Frady was questioned by investigators after turning himself in, but could not be released as a condition of his bond until a company was found to provide an electronic monitoring system.

<!-- P008 -->
Milton Frady’s attorney, Michael Hauptman, said many of the companies were wary of taking on a high-profile case.

<!-- P009 -->
Sentinel Monitoring Corporation, a Marietta-based firm, agreed to provide security late last week.

<!-- P010 -->
Milton Frady will wear an ankle bracelet which is wired directly to the security company. In addition, surveillance cameras will be set up in and around the residence.

<!-- P011 -->
Frady was also ordered to post a $22,400 bond and pay for the cost of deputies transporting him home. No court date has been set for the 1995 charges.

<!-- P012 -->
Meanwhile, authorities say they are still pursuing leads in their investigation into the murder of Levi Frady.

<!-- P013 -->
The second man in the sketch police released last week remains unidentified. Investigators say the man is not considered a suspect but is thought to be a potential witness.

<!-- P014 -->
They said they simply want to talk with him to see if he has any information.

<!-- P015 -->
The man in a third artist sketch, which was never released to the public, has been identified. Special Agent in Charge of the Gainesville Georgia Bureau of Investigation office, Jim Hallman, said agents interviewed the man over the weekend and are pursuing leads from information he provided.

<!-- P016 -->
Hallman declined to identify the man, who was seen in the wooded area near the location where Levi was murdered. The description of the man was provided by a citizen police talked to during a roadblock in the area last week.

<!-- P017 -->
“The case is still progressing,” Hallman said. “We’ve got a number of leads remaining to check, and we’re doing that as quickly as possible.”
