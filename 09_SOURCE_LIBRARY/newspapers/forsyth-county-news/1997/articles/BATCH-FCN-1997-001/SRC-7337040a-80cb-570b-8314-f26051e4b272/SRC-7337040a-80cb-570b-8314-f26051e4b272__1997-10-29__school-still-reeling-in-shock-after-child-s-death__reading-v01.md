## Full extant article text

<!-- P001 -->
Students and faculty of North Forsyth Middle School tried to get back to business as usual Monday. But it wasn’t easy.

<!-- P002 -->
“We are still reeling like this community is,” said principal Jane Fuqua. “We’re in shock.”

<!-- P003 -->
The fun and innocence of the students was shattered last week when the body of sixth grader Levi Frady was found in Dawson Forest.

<!-- P004 -->
Frady disappeared Wednesday, apparently on his way home from a friend’s house.

<!-- P005 -->
On Thursday, family and friends searched frantically in the area surrounding his Burruss Mill home. The only sign of Frady was a bicycle found in a ditch off Little Mill Road, by his twin sister, Laci.

<!-- P006 -->
It wasn’t until three hunters stumbled upon the body of the 11-year-old that Frady’s fate was known.

<!-- P007 -->
[Continued on page 2A under SCHOOL from 1A]

<!-- P008 -->
He was shot to death, the victim of what investigators are calling a murder.

<!-- P009 -->
“I really don’t know what to say,” said Fuqua. She and more than 500 students and faculty attended Frady’s funeral Sunday. “But we’re not ready to just bury the issue.”

<!-- P010 -->
Three psychologists and the school’s counseling staff returned Monday to help those who wanted and needed to talk about the tragedy.

<!-- P011 -->
Parents seemed to have recovered from the initial shock, as was evidenced by the number of children riding the buses.

<!-- P012 -->
Fuqua said Friday, it looked like every parent in the school dropped off and picked up their child. Monday, numbers were back up.

<!-- P013 -->
But Fuqua said the situation isn’t something that will go away over the course of a weekend.

<!-- P014 -->
Regardless of the outcome of law enforcement investigation, the faculty and students will remember Frady every day.

<!-- P015 -->
Plans are under way for a permanent memorial on the grounds of North Forsyth Middle School.

<!-- P016 -->
Fuqua said there are no definite plans on what the memorial will be, but it will rely heavily on student input.

<!-- P017 -->
In the meantime, said Fuqua, the school needs exactly what Frady’s family and the community needs — time and space to heal.
