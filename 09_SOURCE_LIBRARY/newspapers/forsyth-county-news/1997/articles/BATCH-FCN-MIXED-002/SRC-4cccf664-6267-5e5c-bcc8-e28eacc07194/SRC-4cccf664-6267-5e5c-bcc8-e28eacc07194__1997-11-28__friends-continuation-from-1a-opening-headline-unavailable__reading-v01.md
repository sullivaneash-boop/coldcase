Sometimes meeting in groups — sometimes talking one-on-one, Watters says the most important thing is for she and other counselors to let the children share their feelings.

<!-- P004 -->
“We do more listening than talking, and the children talk about how they feel and share memories about Levi. We want to know what life is like for them now.”

<!-- P005 -->
Watters says “children go through the grieving process differently than do adults:” Because Levi’s friends are so young, she says “this may be the first personal loss — where they truly miss someone — which many children have ever experienced.”

<!-- P006 -->
Initially, the grief is “on the surface” she said, but now it surfaces in “more subtle ways.”

<!-- P007 -->
When children lose someone close to them, Watters says it is common for them to channel their sadness in other areas: “They will act out, have less self control or be angrier,” she says, “and they may not even be aware that they are really feeling sad.”

<!-- P008 -->
So, parents and teachers can sometimes mistake the change in behavior and not realize “it’s really grief.”

<!-- P009 -->
To help, Watters says counselors try to “reassure children that their feelings are natural” and have assigned many of the students peer and adult mentors.

<!-- P010 -->
Watters says the mentors undergo thorough background screenings and are available to “spend time with the kids and do special things” to help them cope.

<!-- P011 -->
Although she describes the atmosphere as one of “edginess right now,” she doesn’t see the “fear and shock” so apparent in the early aftermath of Levi’s slaying.

<!-- P012 -->
“They would like to know what happened — to know why, but mostly they just talk about missing Levi and what he was like.”

<!-- P013 -->
Even though Watters says most of the children don’t seem overly fearful, she says many seem “frustrated” that their parents are afraid to let them go outside and play as they did before.

<!-- P014 -->
“Their lifestyles have changed,” she said. “Whereas kids used to run loose and do what they wanted, now they can’t play in the woods, and are therefore more restricted.”

<!-- P015 -->
Watters says the school has no immediate plans for a memorial service for Levi, although she expects some type of permanent memorial to his life will eventually be designated.

<!-- P016 -->
“Right now, we’re trying to get some distance between the children and Levi’s death and let things normalize. To hold a service at this point, she says, would be too upsetting. “Adults can handle that, but children can’t.”
