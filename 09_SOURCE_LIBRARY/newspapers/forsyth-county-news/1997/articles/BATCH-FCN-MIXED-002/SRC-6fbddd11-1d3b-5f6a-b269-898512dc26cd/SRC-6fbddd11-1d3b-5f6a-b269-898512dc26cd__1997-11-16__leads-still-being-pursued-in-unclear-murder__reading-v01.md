## Full extant source text

<!-- P001 -->
Is there a random killer on the loose — one who kills for no reason? Early in the investigation into Levi Frady’s murder, officials tried to ease parents’ fears that someone might be stalking Forsyth County children.

<!-- P002 -->
Just days after the 11-year-old’s body was found, Dawson County Sheriff Billy Carlisle told worried parents that statistics in child homicide cases indicate that young victims usually die at the hands of someone they know.

<!-- P003 -->
But with time passing without an arrest in the murder, Levi’s maternal grandmother questions whether other children in Forsyth are safe from Levi’s assailant.

<!-- P004 -->
In a written statement, Janice Hamby poses the question: “Are your children safe at the bus stop? There’s a killer out there somewhere,” she writes. “They have killed once, and it will be easier the next time.”

<!-- P005 -->
Even when Carlisle generalized about child murders, he was quick to add that no judgment was being made about Levi’s murder specifically, saying he did not know for certain whether Levi had known his killer.

<!-- P006 -->
The grandmother wonders whether parents shouldn’t be taking extra precautions until the murderer is found, saying she thinks it’s possible Levi did not know his killer. “We just don’t know right now. It could have happened either way,” she said.

<!-- P007 -->
Because of the uncertainty, she doesn’t want parents to have a false sense of security. “I don’t want to scare anyone. I just want them to be cautious until the murderer is found. If one child can be killed, another child could be killed.

<!-- P008 -->
“A child should be allowed to play,” she writes, but points out that parents should not assume the person living next door or down the street is safe. “Do you (really) know your neighbor?”
