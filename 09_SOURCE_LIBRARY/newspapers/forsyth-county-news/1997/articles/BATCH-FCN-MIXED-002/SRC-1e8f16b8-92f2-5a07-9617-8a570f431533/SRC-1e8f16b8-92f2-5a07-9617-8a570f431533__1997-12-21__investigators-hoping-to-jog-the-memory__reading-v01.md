## Full extant source text

<!-- P001 -->
During a Friday afternoon press conference, agents investigating the murder of young Levi Frady echoed the slain 11-year-old’s mother’s belief that “someone must have seen or heard something” the evening Levi disappeared.

<!-- P002 -->
Marilyn Parkman ended the family’s plea for help in bringing her son’s killer to justice with the words, “Please call.”

<!-- P003 -->
To help “jog the memory” of anyone who may have information in the case, Georgia Bureau of Investigation spokesperson John Bankhead wants to remind area residents of Oct. 22 — the day Levi went out for a ride on his bike.

<!-- P004 -->
“It was the night of the fifth game of the World Series,” he said, “and it was a Wednesday night, when people may have been going to prayer service.”

<!-- P005 -->
Perhaps most importantly, since Daylight Saving’s Time was still in effect, Bankhead pointed out that investigators believe Levi came into contact with his assailant(s) before the sun went down — sometime between 6 and 7 p.m.

<!-- P006 -->
That’s significant, he said, because it means someone “could have seen” the perpetrator on Little Mill Road before or after Levi was abducted. It’s also possible, he said, that someone noticed “a young boy in a car” elsewhere that evening.

<!-- P007 -->
He’s hoping the reminder will cause everyone to think back to that day, recall what he or she was doing and possibly remember even something seemingly insignificant which might help investigators solve the case.

<!-- P008 -->
Bankhead told reporters that Levi’s father, Milton Frady, who initially refused to come forward and answer questions about his son’s disappearance, had now “pretty much been ruled out” as a suspect.

<!-- P009 -->
When asked whether others in the family continue to be possible suspects, Bankhead replied, “I don’t feel that’s a significant aspect.”

<!-- P010 -->
As for the speculation that Levi was murdered as a means of retaliation against someone he was close to, the GBI spokesman would only say that agents “are not ruling out any possibility at this point.”

<!-- P011 -->
Bankhead said investigators from several regional GBI offices are working to solve the case and have thus far compiled 30 volumes of case files and pursued more than 500 leads.

<!-- P012 -->
Special Agent-in-Charge of the Gainesville GBI region, Jim Hallman, said agents have polygraphed at least 20 individuals in the case to date. He would not reveal the identity of those questioned or say whether anyone failed the exam.

<!-- P013 -->
Hallman and two other investigators returned last week from a lengthy consultation with behavioral scientists and other child abduction experts at the FBI training center in Virginia.

<!-- P014 -->
Anyone with any information is asked to call the Dawson County hotline at 800-432-9284, the GBI at 535-5423 or Forsyth County Crime Stoppers at 888-7308. For those who prefer not to speak directly to law enforcement, write to: Levi Frady Case, P.O. Box 229, Dawsonville, Ga. 30534.
