## Full extant source text

<!-- P001 -->
DEAR EDITOR:

<!-- P002 -->
A letter appeared in your Sunday, April 26 issue written by the aunt of Levi Frady, the 11 year-old Forsyth County boy whose body was found in Dawson Forest in October. I wanted to address some concerns she raised in this letter.

<!-- P003 -->
First of all, I want to assure Ms. Patrick and all of the relatives and friends of this child that the Sheriff’s Office is doing everything humanly possible to apprehend the person or persons responsible for his murder. Although Levi Frady did live in Forsyth County, the crime of murder did occur in Dawson County. Under the law, a crime must be investigated by the county authorities in the county where it occurred. When more than one county is involved in a crime, a common practice in law enforcement is to ask for assistance from a larger agency. That agency in this case is the Georgia Bureau of Investigation (G.B.I.

<!-- P004 -->
The G.B.I. is now the lead investigating agency, with Dawson County assisting. The Forsyth County Sheriff’s Office is only a support agency in this investigation, unlike the investigation of the decapitated man. In that case, we were the lead investigating agency. At the beginning of the investigation, all of the evidence was located in Forsyth County.

<!-- P005 -->
We now have an investigator who is working with the G.B.I. on a full-time basis, following up on leads given to him. The G.B.I. and Dawson County authorities are also very concerned about the murder of this child and have done a professional job with the investigation.

<!-- P006 -->
Also in this letter, mention was made of the disagreements between the County Commission and the Sheriff taking time away from this investigation. The Commissioners have been nothing but supportive throughout the investigation. They contributed money towards a reward fund and Chairman Bill Jenkins has also verbally given me permission to use whatever overtime hours we may need to solve this horrible crime. In no way has any disagreement between the Commissioners and the Sheriff hampered the investigation of the murder of Levi Frady.

<!-- P007 -->
The death of Levi Frady never leaves my mind. Unfortunately, there are no deadlines on investigations. Many times crimes are solved only when the perpetrator’s conscious begins to wear down and he or she must tell someone to alleviate themselves of the heavy burden of guilt.

<!-- P008 -->
I promise we will continue to work diligently to solve this murder. One day someone will be brought to justice and pay for the crime that has been committed against one of God’s children.

<!-- P009 -->
Denny Hendrix
Sheriff, Forsyth County
